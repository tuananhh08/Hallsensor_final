## ABSTRACT

Magnetic manipulation provides a promising approach to enhancing the maneuverability and safety of capsule robots (CR). Due to its advantages of human harmlessness and low latency, magnetic signal-based positioning technology is regarded as the most promising CR localization method. However, traditional magnetic localiza­ tion approaches based on optimization algorithms have initial values sensitivity and error accumulation issues especially in environments with multiple magnetic sources. To address this challenge, a neural network based on magnetic positioning is proposed, which integrates physics-based principles with transfer learning. The method builds a ResNet-based prediction model with multi-scale convolution and channel attention mechanism to bolster system resilience. A loss function based on physical information and designed with a magnetic field analysis model is provided, which enhances the interpretability of model inference. A transfer training strategy involving pre-training with physical model data and fine-tuning with real data is presented, which achieves high posi­ tioning accuracy even when data is limited. The efficacy of the localization methodology is validated in an ex vivo pig stomach, resulting in an average position error and angle error of 2.90 mm and 1.26◦, respectively, with a single positioning time of 0.34 ms. The results demonstrate the promising potential of this technology for translation into clinical practice.

For the localization of magnetic capsule robots (MCR), researchers have proposed many robust and effective methods and conducted in- depth studies. Some researchers have improved the general posi­ tioning method and applied it to micro-medical devices: such as vision localization [5], Radio Frequency (RF) localization [6] and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) localization [7], However, the weak surface texture of the gastrointestinal tract and its deformable, hollow structural characteristics interfere with visual feature extraction and radio fre­ quency signal reception. Simultaneously, due to factors such as error accumulation and magnetic interference, IMU cannot achieve inde­ pendent positioning and are typically employed as auxiliary means to other positioning methods. ## 1. Introduction

The magnetic control capsule robot is a new type of wireless micro medical robot used for gastrointestinal examination. It collects images of the stomach through a miniature camera to assist in diagnosis and treatment, which can avoid the pain and potential wounds that may be brought by traditional cable gastroscopy, as well as the limited scope of passive capsule endoscopy [1]. Precise control of capsule robot requires rapid feedback on its real-time position [2,3], making real-time accurate localization of the capsule a critical prerequisite for effective clinical operation [4]. Moreover, real-time location information is essential for recording the images collected. This not only supports the re- examination and targeted treatment of gastric lesions but also signifi­ cantly reduces the missed detection rate, improves the examination accuracy and operational space, providing a core guarantee for the reliable clinical application of capsule robots. Magnetic positioning technology typically involves one or more magnetic sources and sensor modules. To achieve the purpose of mag­ netic drive, permanent magnets are embedded inside the capsule robot, and the corresponding control magnetic sources and sensor arrays are arranged outside the body [12]. Because the simultaneous magnetic drive and positioning of capsule robots involve multi-source magnetic field modeling and decoupling [13], it remains a challenging task at present. Aiming at the compatibility problem of magnetic positioning and magnetic drive, Natali et al. [14] proposed a method of magnetic field decoupling through pre-calculated magnetic field diagrams, achieving 3D positioning of small magnetic drive medical devices. Son et al. [15] performed the quadratic differentiation of the Z-axis differ­ ential magnetic field to minimize the influence of the driving magnetic field during the positioning process. Shi et al. [16] in order to improve the positioning accuracy of the magnetic-controlled capsule robot, the magnetic field of the external permanent magnet was estimated by op­ tical tracking to achieve decoupling of the internal and external per­ manent magnets. The effectiveness of the method was verified through dynamic experiments and static experiments. Decoupling the inner and outer permanent magnets through differential methods is effective, but the influence of the permanent magnet model error on the positioning accuracy is ignored. Hua et al. [17] designed a double-layer magnetic sensor array that can counteract the magnetic field interference of the external driving permanent magnet at a specific angle to achieve three- degree-of-freedom positioning. Zeng et al. [18] deduced the analytical relationship between the driving magnetic field and the magnetically controlled helical robot, decoupled the magnetic field component caused by the robot from the driving magnetic field, and achieved the positioning estimation of the permanent magnet. However, the complexity of the analytical model greatly limited the computational speed. Zhang et al. [19] developed a magnetically controlled capsule robot integrated with an IMU. By data fusion with Hall sensor data, its 6- D precise positioning was achieved. However, the space occupation of the IMU within the capsule robot is a problem that needs to be considered. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

The magnetic positioning solution algorithms for permanent mag­ nets can be classified into the following types based on different modeling methods. To solve the inverse problem of permanent magnet position calculation, the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) algorithm has been widely applied in the pose solution based on the magnetic dipole model (MDM) due to its advantages of fast calculation speed and high calcu­ lation accuracy [16,20]. As an iterative method for nonlinear optimi­ zation problems, its positioning accuracy is affected by the accuracy of the initial solution, and at the same time, there is the problem of being unable to escape from the local optimal solution. Intelligent optimiza­ tion algorithms do not require the continuity of the fitness function and can perform global optimization through multiple iterations. They also demonstrate excellent performance in solving analytical models [21]. However, as a computationally intensive algorithm, inappropriate Set­ tings can still lead to local optima and slow computation. Based on this, researchers have proposed a variety of methods to improve and constrain the classical schemes. Song et al. [22] provided constraints and initial values for solving the analytical model based on the particle swarm algorithm through the magnetic dipole model and the LM algo­ rithm, and achieved the six-degree-of-freedom positioning of the radi­ ally magnetized toroidal permanent magnet. Ye et al. [23] proposed a method based on Improved Artificial Bee Colony (IABC) algorithm for solving the magnetic dipole model, which has higher positioning accu­ racy and faster convergence speed compared with the LM algorithm and other classic intelligent optimization algorithms. Zhang et al. [24] [URL 🔗](#page-0)

proposed a solution method based on a hybrid model. The initial values were solved through the magnetic dipole model and the particle swarm optimization algorithm, and were used as the initial values for solving the analytical model by the LM algorithm. As the movement speed of the permanent magnet increases, the positioning accuracy based on the optimization method decreases. At this time, the method based on Kalman filtering is proposed and applied to magnetic tracking [25]. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

In recent years, methods based on deep learning [12] have been used in the field of magnetic positioning, but they have the problem of being [URL 🔗](#page-0)

sensitive to the amount of data. Qin et al. [26] [URL 🔗](#page-0)

[27] trained the ResNet network to estimate the initial position of the capsule robot using 5,312 sets of sampling data, and fused the estimated value of the network with the calculated value of LM using the adaptive weighting algorithm. The positioning accuracy was 6.09mm. Due to the error between the data source and the true value of the above- mentioned neural network dataset and the insufficient data volume, the positioning accuracy is limited and can only provide the initial value for the LM algorithm. Wang et al. Su et al. While recent studies such as Wang et al. [28] and Su et al. [29] have successfully applied transfer learning and few-shot techniques to magnetic localization, they pre­ dominantly rely on purely data-driven feature extraction. These ap­ proaches typically address single-source environments or treat multi- source interference merely as unstructured noise, which can lead to non-physical trajectory jumps under strong dynamic interference. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

To overcome these limitations and explicitly address the multi- source coupling challenge, we propose a unified physics-informed framework. The main contributions of this work are highlighted as follows:

(1) To isolate the weak capsule signal (IPM) from the dominant external driving field (EPM), we construct a 6-channel input represen­ tation combining the 3-axis noise-suppressed sensor data with the 3-axis theoretical EPM components. By integrating multi-scale convolutions and a channel attention mechanism, the network adaptively weights these explicitly paired channels, achieving feature-space decoupling of the coupled magnetic fields.

(2) The equivalent current model is embedded as a physical consis­ tency loss, ensuring that the network's 5-DoF pose predictions strictly adhere to spatial electromagnetic distributions, improving the physical reliability of the localization results.

(3) The ModNet designed within this framework achieves physical alignment between sensor hardware data and EPM simulation data. Furthermore, our phased transfer learning strategy adapts the unified framework to real-world hardware imperfections without overwriting the pre-trained physical priors, effectively bridging the domain gap between analytical simulations and practical clinical applications.

LocNet is designed to estimate the 5-DoF pose of the capsule robot. Since the three components of the magnetic sensor array data exhibit a “channel sequence length” structure, a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN) is adopted to process this data structure, while re­ sidual connections are introduced to mitigate the issue of gradient vanishing in deeper layers.

In the localization system of the magnetic control capsule robot, both the sensor measurement data and the status of the EPM are necessary conditions for the position calculation of the capsule robot. To explicitly address the multi-source magnetic coupling problem, LocNet employs a structurally aligned 6-channel input. This input concatenates the 3-axis noise-suppressed magnetic induction components (output from Mod­ Net) with the 3-axis theoretical components of the EPM. ## 4.6. Compared with other works

In this section, the proposed CRLocNet is compared with the state-of- the-art magnetic localization methods, where the results summarized in Table 4. Compared to traditional optimization-based algorithms (e.g., LM and IABC) and hybrid neural networks (e.g., ResNet-LM and BP-LM), CRLocNet achieves a superior balance of precision and efficiency, yielding the lowest average position error of 2.90 mm and an orientation error of 1.26◦ within a sub-millisecond execution time. This superior performance is primarily attributed to the seamless integration of the CMDM and the physics-alignment capability of ModNet. Specifically, the CMDM effectively compensates for near-field non-linear distortions, while the ModNet suppresses environmental noise to isolate and refine the IPM signals essential for high-precision localization. Furthermore, the training strategy based on transfer learning effectively mitigates the overfitting issue in small-sample training. The incorporation of physical consistency loss leads to the restriction of the solution space within the physically valid domain, ensures the generation of robust and inter­ pretable predictions, which are generally unattainable by purely data- driven architectures. [URL 🔗](#page-0)

Crucially, while many existing studies based on deep learning focus on the localization of a single permanent magnet, our approach specif­ ically addresses the challenge of multi-source magnetic coupling, where the interference between the driving EPM and the target IPM signifi­ cantly complicates signal decoupling. By reformulating the non-linear inverse problem into a learning task guided by a forward physical model, CRLocNet successfully maintains high-precision tracking under magnetic actuation. This capability to handle multi-source interference makes our method inherently more challenging and technically advanced than traditional single-magnet trackers, offering broader application prospects for active magnetic capsule robots in complex clinical gastrointestinal environments.

## 5. Conclusion and future works

In this work, a transfer learning-based physics-informed neural network approach for real-time localization is proposed and applied to magnetically controlled capsule robots. For the permanent magnet- controlled capsule robot system, factors affecting localization accuracy are analyzed, and a modified magnetic dipole model with reduced measurement error is obtained through magnetic moment correction. While data-driven localization methods are often constrained by dataset scale and resolution, the end-to-end neural network CRLocNet presented in this paper addresses the inverse problem of coupled magnetic models through physics-informed constraints and a transfer learning strategy. Compared with traditional optimization-based methods, it offers the following advantages:(1) High localization accuracy (2.90 mm, 1.26◦), particularly in near-field regions and under interference from external permanent magnets (EPMs). (2) Enhanced robustness in localization. As a feedforward neural network, CRLocNet mitigates the risk of converging to local optima. (3) Strong real-time performance. By replacing iterative optimization with regression, the computational la­ tency is reduced to 0.34 ms. (4) Improved interpretability through the incorporation of physics-informed data and physical consistency con­ straints to guide the learning process. The high accuracy and real-time capability of CRLocNet make it well-suited for synchronous localiza­ tion of capsule robots under magnetic control.

The integration of physical consistency constraints establishes a foundation for debugging and clinical trust. In actual medical proced­ ures, sensor noise or transient magnetic interference may cause purely data-driven models to output non-physical mutations or singularities. By applying physical magnetic field principles in the loss function, CRLocNet ensures that predicted trajectories remain kinematically and physically plausible even under suboptimal conditions. This constraint

not only assists developers in debugging hardware-software mismatches but also enhances the robot system's interpretability and safety, which are critical for ensuring secure medical robot operations. Current research focuses solely on enhancing the positioning accuracy of capsule robots at their current location. Future studies will examine the impact of control system deviations on capsule robot positioning and explore the application of magnetic field continuity principles in magnetically controlled capsule robots to further improve the precision and stability of magnetic localization.
